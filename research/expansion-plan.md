# What we could sell after Google review replies

A working note for us, 2 September 2026. Not a product decision. V1 stays locked: we reply to Google reviews, 99 € TTC / month, WhatsApp to the owner, AI drafts, a person publishes.

The question is what we *could* add later, with the same factory: about 90 % of the work done by software, about 10 % by a person who checks tone and does not let anything stupid go live. Aimed at the same independent shops. Helpful commercially for how they keep clients, and a bit beyond that.

---

## What we already decided, so we do not invent a second company

Two expansion ideas are already in the dossier, and they should stay first in line:

1. **Ask for a review after the visit**, folded into the 99 € once the reply flow actually holds. If we never ask, the rating barely moves and churn eats the MRR (`02-marche-legal.md`).
2. **WhatsApp for the shop’s own customers** (hours, menu, next three Calendar slots). A separate product, after about 40 review clients. No cold writing to their customers without a legal basis (`01-produit.md`).

Everything below is *after* those, or a sharper version of them. We should not open a third factory before the first one has paying shops and a Philippine operator who is not drowning.

That gap is the company. New services should stay inside it. The moment we ask the owner to log into a dashboard every morning, we are competing with $75 software we will not beat on features.

## Why replies alone are not the whole commercial job

Two papers we already use on the public research page, plus a 2025 consumer survey:

- Michael Luca, HBS working paper 12-016 (revised 2016): a **one-star** increase in the displayed Yelp rating is associated with **5–9 % more revenue**, and the effect is for *independents*, not chains. ([HBS item](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=41233), [PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=12-016.pdf))
- Proserpio & Zervas, *Marketing Science* 2017: hotels that start *responding* get about **0.12 stars** more and **12 % more reviews**. Negative reviews get fewer but longer. ([journal](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2017.1043))
- BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025 (n = 1,026 US adults): **96 %** of consumers are open to writing a review; **email** is the request method they say they are most likely to answer (40 %), then **in person** (27 %); for food and drink, **48 %** expect the ask the same day or the next day; people are much more likely to use a business that replies to *all* reviews, not only the good or only the bad; in a blind test, they again preferred an AI-written reply they were not told was AI. ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/))

So: replies matter, volume of new reviews matters more for the rating, and the owner will not do the asking. That is why Ask belongs in the base offer once replies hold. It is also why we should not copy the US “if they said it was great, send them to Google; if not, keep it private” pattern. Google’s Maps policy forbids **selectively soliciting positive reviews**, **incentives**, **on-premises pressure**, **staff review quotas**, and **asking for specific content including a staff name**. We *may* ask everyone, equally, for a genuine review, with no reward. ([Google contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114))

Owner.com’s own summer 2025 product video describes routing happy guests to the Google review page and unhappy ones to a private alert. That is the gating Google names. We should not build it, even if it “works” for them commercially.

## What we found on X

Not a scientific sample. Useful as a picture of what people are selling and complaining about in August–September 2026.

- Review reply is still described as an **operations** problem, not a marketing problem: reviews sit unanswered, nobody reuses them as proof.
- A second, uglier thread: **fake 1-star bursts** used as extortion. We are not a Google-dispute law firm. We could, later, help an owner *report* a review and keep a file. We should not promise to “fix” Google.
- **WhatsApp catalogs** are still how a lot of small sellers in Africa, the Gulf, and South-East Asia actually take orders. Meta launched catalogs in 2019; they are ordinary now, not a novelty.
- **TikTok Shop** conversation on X is almost entirely e-commerce and live selling, not the neighbourhood restaurant. Transferring that playbook to Sant Cugat hospitality would be a different company.
- A wave of “AI agent that books 24/7 on WhatsApp / Instagram / Google” products (Latir, AforIA, SembangBiz, Repswell). They sell the thing we have chosen not to be: full auto, no person. Their existence is a reminder that the market will be noisy. Our difference has to stay the person who publishes.

## Companies that are strong at social commerce, and what transfers

### United States

Podium turned review requests into a **texting and payments** company. Owner.com turned Google traffic into **direct orders** so the restaurant pays less to DoorDash. Shopify Inbox and Meta Shops are for brands that already have a catalogue. TikTok Shop US is growing fast (eMarketer, cited in trade roundups: about **$15.8 B** US GMV in 2025). Almost none of that is a 99 € managed service for a one-location café.

What transfers: **ask after the transaction**, **keep the relationship on the channel the customer already uses**, **do not make the owner learn software**. In Spain the channel is WhatsApp, not SMS.

### South-East Asia

[Sea Limited’s FY 2025 results](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260302039769/en/Sea-Limited-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results): Shopee GMV **$127.4 B**, about **400 million** active buyers and **20 million** sellers. In the Q2 2026 call, live and short-form video were more than **35 %** of physical-goods orders in SEA. Grab is food and grocers plus a super-app. TikTok Shop is the attention-to-cart layer.

What transfers: selling happens **inside the chat or the feed**, not on a website the owner never updates. What does *not* transfer: cash on delivery, reseller armies, and a marketplace we would never own.

### India

Meesho started as WhatsApp resellers and listed in December 2025. The [TechCrunch IPO piece](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/28/softbank-stays-in-as-meesho-606m-ipo-becomes-indias-first-major-e-commerce-listing/) and later earnings describe a pivot to a full marketplace (hundreds of millions of transacting users, ~846k sellers in a January 2026 call). The lesson is slightly uncomfortable: **pure social-commerce reselling did not scale**; they had to become logistics and a real shop.

Around that, a whole industry of WhatsApp “operating systems” for SMBs (WATI, Interakt, AiSensy, Gallabox): shared inbox, broadcast, chatbot, Shopify. That *is* social commerce for a small Indian brand. Meta’s [Cloud API pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing) is per delivered *template* message since 1 July 2025. Service replies inside the 24-hour window have been free; Meta has said it will start charging service messages from **1 October 2026**. Spain marketing rates already went up on 1 July 2026.

What transfers: **WhatsApp is the storefront**. Catalogues, Flows for “book a slot”, a human who closes the odd conversation. What does *not* transfer: blasting marketing templates at a European list without a proper opt-in, or staffing a 24/7 inbox we cannot price.

## A simple test for any new service

We should only add a line if it passes most of this:

| Test | Why |
|---|---|
| Same buyer as the 99 € | We already have WhatsApp trust. A new category (ads, websites, TikTok Shop) is a new sales motion. |
| Same factory | Draft → operator click → maybe owner OK on WhatsApp. If the work is “design a brand” or “run ads”, the 90/10 split dies. |
| Owner stays on WhatsApp | No new app. That is the promise on the site. |
| Legal on a quiet day | GDPR / LSSI, Google fake-engagement rules, Meta templates. If we need a lawyer every week, we are too early. |
| Margin still ≥ 30 % before founder pay | Tokens are cheap. WhatsApp marketing templates, 24/7 coverage, and a second operator shift are not. |
| We can say no to the 1-star case | Anything that publishes to the world still needs a person. |

## Services that look like us

Ranked for *after* v1 is boringly reliable. Prices are guesses to discuss, not an offer.

### 1. Ask — request a review after the visit (base offer, not a new SKU)

**What.** After a meal, a cut, a session, we send *every* recent customer the same short message: “if you want to leave a Google review, here is the link.” No filter on mood. No discount. Not while they are still in the chair (Google now treats on-premises pressure as fake engagement).

**Why commercially.** Replies without new reviews is half a product. BrightLocal: people will write if asked, and F&B wants the ask immediately. Proserpio: responding also *brings* more reviews. Our own research page already prices the 0.12-star × 7 % revenue story.

**90/10.** The message is a template. The operator samples a few a week. The owner is not in the loop unless volume spikes or someone complains.

**Catch.** We cannot invent the phone numbers, and a booking number is not marketing consent. The fastest legal path is a **printed QR / short link** (Google’s own how-to: receipts, table tent, end of a chat). No personal data, no LSSI. Automated WhatsApp/SMS to diners only later, with a logged opt-in. No scraping Google reviewers.

**When.** QR kit as soon as replies are on time. Automated send only with a jurist pass and a consent log.

### 2. Presence — one Google Post a week, written from real 5-star reviews

**What.** A short post on the profile: this week’s dish, this week’s hours, a sentence taken from a real review. Offer / event / update. The [GBP localPosts API](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/posts-data) is the same manager access we already need for replies.

**Why.** Google Q&A as a public section is being wound down (API support ended 3 November 2025, per trade write-ups citing Google’s change). The profile still needs to look alive. BrightLocal: a lot of people watch *the business’s own* video and photos when they shortlist. A dead profile next to a live competitor is a quiet loss of clients we never hear about.

**90/10.** Draft from last week’s 5-stars + hours. Operator publishes or skips. Owner only if we want to announce a closure.

**Catch.** Photos. We said photos are not a v1 product. We could start with text-only posts, or reuse a photo the shop already has on the profile. We should not become a photography agency.

**When.** Same quarter as Ask. Almost no extra access.

**Price, maybe.** Keep it inside 99 € so the profile does not rot, *or* a 129 € “fiche vivante” if Ask + Posts + replies is clearly more work. I would rather keep 99 € honest and not nickel-and-dime.

### 3. Guest WhatsApp — the v2 we already wrote down

**What.** The shop’s public WhatsApp (or a number we run for them) answers “are you open?”, “do you have a table at 21:00?”, “is there gluten-free?”. Next three Calendar / CoverManager slots. Owner gets a ping only when the bot is outside the script.

**Why.** This is the India/SEA lesson that actually fits Spain. People already book and complain on WhatsApp. CoverManager and a few Spanish bots (mesaking and others) are selling 24/7 WhatsApp booking on top of the reservation system. TheFork still takes **€2-ish per diner** when the guest comes from their app. A direct WhatsApp booking that lands in CoverManager is a client relationship the restaurant *owns*.

**90/10.** Scripts first (`07-modeles-couts.md` already says this). Cheap non-reasoning model only if no script matches. Human on the rest. Not grok-4.6 on every “what time do you close”.

**Catch.** This is a second factory: inbound, evenings, no-shows, food allergies. Meta will charge **service messages from 1 October 2026**. We should price it as its own SKU (the 119 € “FAQ mangeurs” already in `10-whatsapp-service.md`), not hide it inside 99 €. We also should not promise 24/7 in v1 of v2. Hours 11–22 with a “we open the chat at 11” auto-reply is more honest.

**When.** After ~40 review clients, as written. Not before the PH operator exists.

### 4. Other platforms — TheFork, Facebook, maybe TripAdvisor

**What.** The same reply factory on the other walls people actually read in Spain. BrightLocal: **74 %** of US consumers still check two or more sites. In Catalonia a restaurant can look loved on Google and abandoned on TheFork.

**Why.** Same buyer, same “you did not answer me” wound. TheFork is also a *sales* channel, not only a review site.

**90/10.** Identical queue, different publisher. TheFork/Tripadvisor APIs are the unknown. Facebook Page reviews are a Graph API problem we do not have today.

**Catch.** We already parked this on purpose for v1. The harder reason: TheFork and TripAdvisor do not give a self-serve write API for owner replies. Facebook ratings can be *read*; posting a reply on that edge is not first-class. Playwright-on-TheFork would break “Publier writes the API”.

**When.** After GBP publish is dull. Facebook comments (see Instagram) before TheFork. Do not promise TheFork publish in year one.

### 5. Instagram comments — same factory, different wall (later than Posts, earlier than catalogues)

**What.** Reply to comments (and inbound DMs) on the shop’s Instagram Professional account the same way we reply to Google: draft, person publishes, owner only on anything that looks like a fight.

**Why.** Spain shops already live there. BrightLocal: people use Instagram as a review-like source. The Graph API for comment replies is documented. It is closer to our muscle than a catalogue.

**Catch.** A Reel can drop 80 comments in an hour. Cap scripted replies. Meta App Review is a real delay (the current app is WhatsApp-only). Not before Google publish is boring.

**When.** After ~40 clients, maybe as a +€39 on the guest-WhatsApp SKU.

### 6. Catalogue — only maybe, only for shops that sell things, not tables

**What.** A WhatsApp catalogue (Meta’s [official commerce guide](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/guides/sell-products-and-services)): florist bunches, bakery week-end orders, workshop slots as “products”. Customer picks, chats to confirm, pays how the shop already pays (Bizum, transfer, in store). We do not become a marketplace.

**Why.** This is the part of Indian/SEA social commerce that is *not* a super-app: a small seller showing stock in the chat they already use. A Sant Cugat florist lives this. A tasting-menu restaurant does not.

**90/10.** Catalogue updates from a weekly WhatsApp with the owner (“these three are out”). Orders that are standard get a template. Odd orders go to the owner.

**Catch.** Inventory lies, payments, returns. Easy to become customer support for the shop. Restrict to one vertical and a cap on SKUs.

**When.** After guest WhatsApp works for that vertical. Not a 2026 promise.

### 7. Monday competitive note — already half-built

**What.** The Monday recap we already send could include: your rating vs the three nearest in the same category, new 1-stars they got, whether their hours look wrong. Scout already reads Places.

**Why.** Owners compare themselves to the street. A recap that only says “we posted 4 replies” is admin. A recap that says “the salon two doors down went from 4.3 to 4.6” is commercial.

**90/10.** Almost 100 % automation. Operator glances.

**Catch.** Do not turn it into a consulting call. One screen, one WhatsApp.

**When.** Cheap. Could ride along as soon as we have 10 clients in one town.

### 8. Recovery — private follow-up after a 1–3 star, if we have the person

**What.** We already ping the owner on 1–3 stars before we publish. Recovery is the next message, to that person, from the shop: “we are sorry, can we put this right?” We can only send it if the restaurant already has that person’s number from a booking or a till, not from Google. Google does not give us the reviewer’s phone.

**Why.** Keeps a client. Sometimes the public reply is theatre; the WhatsApp is the actual relationship.

**90/10.** Draft for the owner. Owner sends or we send after OK. Never automatic.

**Catch.** Easy to look like we are bargaining a review change, which Google forbids if there is an incentive. The message has to be about the meal, not the stars.

### 9. Listing hygiene — hours, attributes, “Google called us”

**What.** Google already auto-calls and WhatsApps businesses to confirm hours and then posts on their behalf ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7690269)). We could be the people who keep hours, holiday closures, and “women-owned / wheelchair / outdoor seating” true, so Maps and the AI overview do not invent them.

**Why.** Wrong hours lose a client before a review ever happens. BrightLocal and every local-SEO thread still start with NAP and hours.

**90/10.** Detect drift vs the website / CoverManager. Draft a change. Operator or owner confirms. We already have manager access.

**Catch.** Q&A as a product is dying. Do not sell “we will fill your Q&A”. Sell “your fiche is true”.

## A possible ladder, not a constitution

Think of it as one relationship that thickens, not a suite.

| Stage | What the shop pays for | Factory | Rough extra work |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Now** | Replies on Google, 99 € | Draft + PH publish + owner OK on 1–3★ | The thing we are building this week |
| **When replies hold** | Same 99 €, plus Ask | Templates to the shop’s own list | WhatsApp/SMS cost, jurist, a sample queue |
| **Same season** | Posts + hygiene + Monday competitive line | One extra draft a week, still GBP | Almost nothing if manager access works |
| **~40 clients** | Guest WhatsApp, own SKU (~119 € as already sketched) | Scripts + human exceptions | Real ops design, Meta billing |
| **Later** | TheFork/Facebook replies | Same queue, new publisher | API or a careful browser |
| **Later, product verticals** | Catalogue | Once a week we would ask the owner which items are out of stock, and we would use templates for ordinary orders | Inventory and payments discipline |

I would not sell a “BabyRock 360” bundle in year one. The US vendors bundle because their sales team needs a $400 number. We can add a line in the same WhatsApp thread when the previous line is obviously working.

## Money, very roughly

Replies are almost all margin (see `16-plan-affaires.md`: ~89 € HT, tokens in cents). New lines that stay on GBP (Posts, hygiene, competitive recap) should look similar.

Ask costs **messages**. Meta utility templates to Spain are cheap compared with marketing templates; a post-visit “here is the review link” should be filed as **utility** if Meta accepts it, and we should not be surprised if they call it marketing. Either way, at tens of messages per shop per month it should still fit under 99 €. If a salon wants 400 asks a month, we cap or we price a pack.

Guest WhatsApp is the first line that can break the 90/10 economically: evenings, allergies, “can we come with a dog”, and from October 2026 a Meta invoice on service messages. That is why it is a second SKU, after we know operator minutes on v1.

I have not built a full P&L for these. We should not, until v1 has a measured avis/jour on real clients (the 0.40 hypothesis in the plan d’affaires).

## What a second, more legal pass changed

A longer note on factory-fit and APIs is in [`adjacent-services.md`](adjacent-services.md). A few points should correct the ladder above, rather than sit in a side file.

**Ask should start as a printed QR and short link, not as WhatsApp to diners.** Google’s own how-to is a review link or QR on the receipt, a thank-you email, or the end of a chat ([Create a link or QR](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815)). A paper QR has no personal data and no LSSI problem. A booking phone number is not marketing consent. Spanish LSSI Art. 21 treats WhatsApp as an electronic commercial channel; AEPD practice is stricter than Meta’s opt-in. Automated “thanks for coming, here is Google” only once the shop has a logged opt-in. Until then, we send the owner the PNG and they print it. That can sit inside the 99 € as soon as replies hold.

**TheFork and TripAdvisor replies stay deferred for a harder reason than “we said so in v1”.** TheFork’s public B2B API can *read* reviews; there is no public POST for the owner reply. TripAdvisor Terra is read; management responses go through their Management Center, one per review, no edit. Playwright-on-TheFork would break our “Publier writes an API” rule. Facebook Page *ratings* can be read (`GET /{page-id}/ratings`); writing a reply on that edge is not a first-class API.

**Catalogues look like social commerce and score badly as a factory.** Maintaining SKUs and photos is merchandising, not draft→publish. Florists might want it one day. A restaurant already has a PDF menu and TheFork. I would not put catalogues on a 2026 promise.

**Instagram comments are a better “same factory” line than I first thought.** `POST` a reply to a comment is a documented Graph call. Volume can spike (a Reel, 80 comments). Cap scripted replies; person on anything that looks like a complaint. Needs a Meta App Review beyond the current “Connect with customers through WhatsApp” use case. After Google publish is dull, before we invent a catalogue.

**Guest WhatsApp needs its own number per shop.** Meta quality score is per phone. One florist’s broadcast must not sink a clinic. The 119 € SKU has to carry a WABA, not share Rosalia’s.

**Competitive monitoring is a Monday line, not a product.** Nearby Search Enterprise is $35 / 1,000. Four calls a month is noise. Do not sell “you will rank #1”.

**Recovery is a checklist on the 1–3★ ping we already send**, not a second SKU: do not offer to delete the review, do not compensate for stars, call the table if you know them.

A packaging that keeps 99 € locked:

| When | What the owner sees | Money |
|---|---|---|
| Soon after publish works | QR kit, holiday-hours ping, optional weekly Post from a 5★ | Included |
| Same moment | 1–3★ ping includes “do not bargain the stars” | Included (prompt) |
| ~40 clients | Shop’s own WhatsApp: FAQ + next 3 Calendar slots | ~119 € TTC, own number |
| After that | IG/FB comments, same QC, volume cap | +€39 or inside 119 if volume is low |
| Not sold | Gating, paid stars, cold diner WhatsApp, “we remove 1★s”, TheFork/TA in year one, catalogues, 80 directories | Policy or wrong factory |
