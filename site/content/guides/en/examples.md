## title
Google review response examples you can use this week

## dek
People look up “google review responses” when they have a blank box and two minutes. Here are replies you can adapt. Change the detail. Do not paste the same paragraph under every review.

## impact_label
Why it matters

## impact
Google tells businesses that a helpful reply shows you are paying attention. The next customer often reads the owner’s words before they read the original stars. A copy-paste block that could sit under any listing does the opposite.

## body
Write in the language of the review. Name one thing they actually said. Keep it short. These Google review response examples only work if you change the detail; Google still checks every reply before it goes live.

## 5-star Google review response examples

After a named dish or service:

“Thank you, Marta. Glad the cut held through the week. We will tell Ana you said hello.”

If they left stars and no text, stay short. Do not invent a dish they did not mention.

## 4-star and mixed Google review responses

A 4-star that mentions a wait:

“Thank you for coming in, and for the note about Saturday’s wait. We had a full book and we were slower than we like. Next time ask for the window table if you want a quieter slot.”

A 3-star that is mixed:

“Thank you for writing this. You are right that the coffee was late. The pastry note helps. If you come back, ask at the bar and we will look after you.”

## Negative Google review response examples

A 1-star or 2-star, when you still need the facts:

“Thank you for telling us. I want to understand what happened with the bill. Please write to us on the number on the ticket, or come by, and ask for me. I will look at it today.”

For the sequence (wait, name the problem, take it off the listing), use the [guide on how to respond to negative Google reviews]([[negative]]).

## What to leave out

A defence of the staff in public, a medical claim, a free dessert you have not agreed, and the sentence “if you enjoyed it please update your review.” Asking someone to change a score in public is a bad look and sits close to the rules Google already has on incentives.

If you cannot write today, write tomorrow. An empty listing is worse than a reply that is one day late. Save three of these in a note on your phone, then change the names and the fact every time.
